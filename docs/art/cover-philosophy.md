# Faceted Ascent

**A visual philosophy: the world as a cross-section, read from the ground up.**

Faceted Ascent treats space as a column of strata. Every image is a vertical slice through a world: the ground sits
at the bottom as a sediment of overlapping ridges, the air above is a stack of calm color bands, and the dark at the
top is just one more layer. The eye reads it the way you read a geological core, from bottom to top and from near to
far. Nothing is a picture of a place. It is a measurement of one, drawn with the patience of a survey plate made by
someone who has spent a lifetime learning to see.

Form is built from flat facets only. Mountains are triangles that agree on a single light source, clouds are stacked
lozenges, and planets are discs cut by clean ellipses. There are no textures and no noise for its own sake. Each
plane of color is chosen and placed by hand, and the work should look as if every vertex had been moved a hundred
times until it settled. Depth comes from atmosphere alone: each layer back is a step paler and a step closer to the
color of the sky, so distance is a gradient you could measure.

Color is a narrow, warm-to-cold sequence. It runs from dusty greens and ochres at the base, through haze blues and
pale cream at the horizon, to indigo and a near-black violet above. One saturated accent is allowed in the whole
composition, small enough to cover a thumbnail and strong enough to hold the frame. It is the only warm-red object
on the page, so the eye finds it instantly and comes back to it. The palette is calibrated with painstaking care: no
two neighbouring values compete, and every band hands off to the next without a seam.

Motion is drawn as notation. A path is a dotted line with even spacing, and a maneuver is a loop drawn as a perfect
curve. Small ticks, crosshairs and dashed circles mark where something was meant to happen. These marks are thin,
regular and slightly clinical, like instrument traces laid over a landscape painting. Their repetition rewards a
long look: the spacing never drifts, the circles close exactly, and the ticks line up on one invisible grid.

Text is a whisper at the margins: altitude figures along one edge, a figure number, and a few labels set in a thin
monospace face, small and widely tracked. It never explains anything. It only adds to the sense that this is a plate
from an atlas of an imaginary discipline. The center of the frame is kept open and quiet, a clear field of sky
where something else can stand. The finished piece should feel meticulously crafted, the product of deep expertise
and master-level execution, something a viewer could study for a long time and keep finding more in.
